What is sprint capacity?

Sprint capacity is the amount of time your team can actually spend on sprint work. It is always lower than headcount times days, because people take time off, work part-time, and spend part of every day in meetings, reviews and support.

How the calculation works

Person-days = (Sprint days − Days off) × Availability %

Focus hours = Person-days × Hours per day × Focus factor

If you add past velocity, ShipKit scales it to this sprint's capacity:

Forecast points = Past velocity × (Person-days now ÷ Past person-days)

Example: your team averaged 40 points in sprints with 45 person-days. This sprint has 39 person-days, so the forecast is 40 × 39 ÷ 45 ≈ 35 points.

How to use it in sprint planning

  1. Set the sprint length and working hours. Leave out public holidays from the working days.
  2. Add each team member with their availability (for example 50% for someone split across two teams) and planned days off.
  3. Pick a focus factor. 60% to 80% is typical. Start at 70% and adjust after a few sprints.
  4. Commit to slightly less than the forecast to leave room for bugs and surprises.

Frequently asked questions

What focus factor should I use?

Most teams land between 60% and 80%. New teams, teams with heavy support duty, or teams with many meetings should start lower.

Should I plan in hours or story points?

Many teams forecast in story points using past velocity and use hours only as a sanity check. This calculator shows both so you can pick.

How do I count someone who is part-time?

Set their availability percentage. Someone working 3 days a week is about 60%.

Does this replace velocity?

No. It adjusts your past velocity for the capacity you have this sprint, which makes the forecast more realistic when people are away.
